5. What changed & recommended next step

On the surface this was a better week: the same money bought a similar amount of traffic and the conversion count went from 10 to 16, cutting cost per conversion from $11.29 to $6.90. The important caveat is that most of that gain is not real. Ten of the sixteen conversions come from a "Contact" tag that fires whenever a page loads, which Google itself has flagged as needing attention, and a fourth tag is outright misconfigured. Once those are set aside, the week delivered one booked appointment and five phone calls from the ads — six real, high-intent leads for about $110, or roughly $18 each. That is a genuinely reasonable number for this business, and it is the number worth reporting to anyone who asks how the advertising is doing.

Two things moved in the right direction independently of the ads. Website traffic grew another 39% in active users, with Direct visits now the biggest single channel — a pattern that usually means people are hearing the name somewhere and typing it in. And Bing's AI-citation count climbed again to 109 in 30 days, roughly four times the July baseline, meaning AI assistants are recommending the business far more often than they were two months ago. A small but notable first: ChatGPT sent 2 visits to the site this week, and Google Analytics now tracks that as its own channel.

Two things need a look. Website phone taps dropped from 23 to 4 in a week when traffic was flat — that pattern usually points at a broken tracking tag rather than a real change in customer behaviour. And the two pages built specifically to attract this kind of attention, the Denver guide and the Spanish shop page, still are not being found; this week's check turned up a plausible reason, which is that the site's sitemap has not been regenerated since June 27 and omits nearly all of the guide pages.

Recommended next steps — conservative, in priority order:
  1. Fix the conversion tracking before judging the numbers. The "Contact" tag should fire on an actual form submission, not a page load, and the "Book appointment" action is flagged Misconfigured. Until these are corrected, the reported conversion count will keep overstating results. This is a developer task, not an account-settings change.
  2. Test the click-to-call button on a mobile phone. Five minutes of manual checking will confirm whether the drop from 23 to 4 phone taps is a tracking fault or genuine. Phone calls are one of the two things the business most wants.
  3. On the Aug 12 ads review (now passed): the case for keeping "Search – DNA Testing Denver" running at $15/day still holds — two consecutive weeks have produced a booking plus phone leads at a defensible cost. Budget pressure eased this week (13.97% of top-of-page share lost to budget, down from 26%), so there is less urgency to raise the budget than last week suggested. Recommend holding at $15/day for now and revisiting once conversion tracking is trustworthy.
  4. Regenerate and resubmit the sitemap. It is seven weeks stale and missing 29 of 30 guide pages plus the Spanish shop. This is the cheapest available fix for the pages that have not been getting cited.
  5. Free win, still outstanding: the campaign's "Ad strength: poor" flag. Adding headline and description variants would likely lift CTR at no extra spend.

As always, this report made no changes to any campaign, tag, budget, or site setting. Everything above is observation and recommendation only.
