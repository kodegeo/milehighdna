5. What changed & recommended next step

It was a slower week, and the slowdown was broad rather than specific. Website visits fell 26%, paid visits fell 32%, organic visits fell 34%, and phone clicks and bookings fell with them. When paid and organic traffic — which have nothing to do with each other mechanically — drop by similar amounts in the same week, the usual explanation is simply that fewer people were looking that week, not that something broke. Nothing in the account or on the website was changed, and every tracking tag that was working last week is still working.

The important number barely moved. Each booked appointment cost $34.47 in advertising this week, against $29.81 last week. Across the two weeks together that averages about $32 per booked appointment. Adding phone calls, the cost of a genuine lead was $17.24 this week and $17.06 last week — a difference of 18 cents. This is the measure the account was set up to produce, and for a business where a prenatal test carries the highest margin, roughly $32 to land a booked appointment continues to look workable. Two weeks is still a small sample, and it should not be treated as settled.

One thing genuinely changed and is worth understanding. The ads were shown to 1,020 people this week, up from 825 — nearly 200 more — yet were clicked 72 times instead of 98. The click-through rate fell from 11.86% to 7.06%, and the price of each click rose 18% to $1.44. More exposure, less interest, higher price. That combination typically means the ads are now appearing for searches that are a looser fit, or that a competitor has started bidding on the same terms. It cannot be diagnosed from the summary screens this report reads, and one week is not enough to act on.

Recommended next step — one thing, and it is a look, not a change. Open the search terms report for the Search – DNA Testing Denver campaign and read the last two weeks side by side. The question to answer: are the extra 200 impressions coming from searches that actually describe what the business sells? If they are irrelevant searches, that explains the whole picture and the fix is a handful of negative keywords. If the search terms look the same as before, then a competitor has likely entered the auction and the sensible response is to do nothing and watch. Do not change the budget or the bid strategy this week. Cost per booked appointment is stable, spend came in under budget for the first time in weeks, and there is no evidence that anything is wrong with the campaign itself.

Two housekeeping items, unchanged from last week. The "Contact" tag that fires on page load still accounts for two-thirds of the reported conversion number and makes the headline figure misleading to anyone reading it cold; it is worth fixing or removing. And Bing Webmaster Tools has now been signed out for four weeks, which means a month of AI-citation data — the one place where the site was outperforming its baseline by roughly 4× — has gone unrecorded. Signing back in takes a minute and restores the whole trend.
