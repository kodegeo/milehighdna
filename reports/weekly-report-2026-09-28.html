5. What changed & recommended next step

The ads are doing their job; the bookings are not following. This week the ads were cheaper per click, more people clicked when they saw them, and more paid visitors reached the site than in any recent week — and they stayed longer. Yet only one booking came in. Bookings have now dropped four weeks running (4, 3, 2, 1) while paid traffic went up. When traffic rises and bookings fall, the most likely causes sit on the website or in the booking step, not in Google Ads.

This is probably not a tracking error. The Calendly booking tag moved from "Needs attention" to "Active" this week, and GA4 and Google Ads both counted exactly one booking. The simplest cross-check is Calendly itself: if Calendly shows more than one appointment booked Sep 21–27, the gap is tracking; if it shows one, the drop is real.

Recommended next step (this week):

  1. Check Calendly's own booking count for Sep 21–27 and compare it with the 1 booking reported here (5 minutes).
  2. Do a test booking on a phone and a computer — homepage → booking page → Calendly → confirmation — and tap the phone number on mobile. This was recommended last week; if it has already been done and everything worked, note that and skip it.
  3. K and Cynthia should hold the budget conversation flagged last week, now that cost per booking has been above $50 two weeks running. No change is recommended until steps 1–2 are done: if the booking flow is broken, fixing it is the answer; if it works and Calendly confirms only one booking, then options like trimming the budget or pausing for a week are worth discussing.

Watch, don't act: Organic Google visits fell 37% in one week, and Bing AI citations have slowed to about half the July baseline pace with none since September 16. Neither is actionable yet; both should be checked again next week.
