Read the drop in conversions carefully. Reported conversions halved from 16 to 8, but this is not a halving of the business. Six of the eight lost conversions came from the "Contact" tag that fires on page load (10 → 4) — a number that was never measuring real contacts in the first place. The account-level banner about bid targets (effective Aug 17) remains informational only — this campaign uses Maximize clicks with no CPA or ROAS target. No account changes were made.

5. What changed & recommended next step

On paper this looks like a bad week: reported conversions fell from 16 to 8 and cost per conversion more than doubled. Most of that is an accounting illusion. Six of the eight "lost" conversions came from the broken Contact tag that fires whenever a page loads — a number that was never measuring anything real, and which happened to run hot last week and cooler this week. The honest comparison is the one that strips it out, and it tells a milder but still negative story: the week produced 4 genuine high-intent leads (1 booked appointment, 3 phone calls) versus 6 last week, on about $8 more spend. Cost per real lead went from roughly $18 to roughly $30.

Underneath, the ads behaved differently this week. Google showed them 12% more often but got 6.5% fewer clicks, so click-through rate fell from 10.35% to 8.66% and the average cost per click rose 15 cents. That pattern — more impressions, worse click rate, higher price — usually means the ads started appearing on a wider and less relevant set of searches. The campaign's serving status did improve from "Eligible (Limited)" to plain "Eligible", but budget pressure ticked up rather than down (16.83% of top-of-page share lost to budget, versus 13.97%).

Website traffic kept growing — 154 users and 188 sessions, both up — and Direct visits are now nearly half of all traffic, which usually means people are encountering the name somewhere and typing it in. One thing to keep an eye on: every non-Google source went to zero this week. No Facebook, no MapQuest, and the ChatGPT visits that appeared for the first time last week did not return. A single week of zeroes on very small numbers is not alarming on its own, but two weeks in a row would be.

Finally, a reporting note: the Bing Webmaster session signed out mid-collection, so this week has no AI-citation number. That is the metric that has been the brightest spot in recent reports, so it is worth reconnecting before next Monday. The sitemap problem identified last week was re-checked directly in the project files and is unchanged — still dated June 27, still missing 29 of 30 guide pages and the Spanish shop.

Recommended next steps — conservative, in priority order:
  1. Fix the conversion tracking. This is now the third week it has been the top recommendation. Until the "Contact" tag stops firing on page loads and the "Book appointment" action is un-misconfigured, the weekly conversion number will keep swinging for reasons that have nothing to do with the business, exactly as it did this week. This is a developer task, not an account-settings change.
  2. Watch the click-through rate for one more week before acting. CTR fell from 10.35% to 8.66% while impressions rose — a single week is not enough to call it a trend, but if it repeats, the likely cause is the campaign matching broader searches, and the fix would be a search-terms review rather than a budget change.
  3. Hold the budget at $15/day. Two weeks of data at $110–120 producing 4–6 real leads is a defensible cost, but this week moved in the wrong direction, and the conversion data is not yet trustworthy enough to justify spending more. The Aug 12 review point has passed; recommend keeping the current setup and revisiting once tracking is fixed.
  4. Reconnect Bing Webmaster Tools so the AI-citation trend can be picked up again next week. It only takes a sign-in.
  5. Regenerate and resubmit the sitemap. Eight weeks stale, missing 29 of 30 guide pages plus the Spanish shop. Still the cheapest available fix for the pages that are not being found.
  6. Free win, still outstanding: add headline and description variants to the active campaign to address the long-standing "Ad strength: poor" flag — likely to lift CTR at no extra spend, which is exactly what this week needs.

As always, this report made no changes to any campaign, tag, budget, or site setting. Everything above is observation and recommendation only.
