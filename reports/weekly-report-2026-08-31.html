5. What changed & recommended next step

The advertising got noticeably cheaper. Spend fell from $118.80 to $73.39 — a 38% drop — while impressions and clicks were essentially unchanged (828 vs 831, and 70 vs 72). The whole difference is price: the average cost per click fell from $1.65 to $1.05. Budget pressure eased at the same time, with the share of top-of-page impressions lost to budget halving from 16.8% to 8.2%. Nothing was changed in the account, so this is the auction being less competitive, not a decision anyone made. It is a genuinely good week for ad efficiency.

The measurement, however, has gone from unreliable to broken. Google Ads reports 11 conversions and its own insight panel is celebrating a "37.5% increase". Ignore it. All three extra conversions — and more — came from the "Contact" tag that fires whenever a page loads, which went from 4 to 9. The actions that count real bookings both now read Misconfigured, and the Calendly one specifically got worse this week (it was "Needs attention" seven days ago). They recorded zero bookings against $73.39 of spend. Meanwhile the website's tap-to-call event, phone_click, recorded zero for the first time — it has never been zero before. The only working lead counter left is Google's own "Calls from ads", which logged 2.

So did the business have a bad week, or just a badly measured one? Probably the latter, at least partly. GA4 recorded 3 book_appointment events — up from 1 — which means people did book. Google Ads simply is not seeing them. The honest summary is that the week produced at least 2 phone calls and 3 website bookings, but there is no longer any trustworthy way to connect bookings back to the ads. Cost per booked appointment — the number this account exists to prove out — cannot be calculated at all this week. That is now the single most important problem, and it has been the top recommendation for four consecutive reports.

Two clear positives. The sitemap has been regenerated: it went from 45 URLs dated June 27 to 112 URLs dated August 27, and now includes all 30 guide pages plus the Spanish shop and /guides/dna-testing-denver, both of which had been missing for four reports. Organic search visits grew for a second straight week (36 → 43), and ChatGPT referrals returned after a week away. Set against that, GA4's headline traffic numbers should be read with suspicion this week — a burst of what looks like bot traffic (70% of all events, twenty events per session, six seconds of engagement, visitors from Singapore, Dubai and Albania) has inflated event counts by 128%.

Recommended next steps — conservative, in priority order

  1. Fix the two booking conversion actions. Both "Booked Appointment (Calendly)" and "Book appointment" are now Misconfigured and returned zero, while GA4 recorded 3 real bookings. This is a developer task on the website tags, not an account-settings change. Until it is fixed, the cost-per-booked-appointment question that this whole ad test was meant to answer cannot be answered.
  2. Check why phone_click stopped firing. A metric going from 5 to 0 in one week almost always means a tag broke, not that the phone stopped ringing. Likely a change to the tap-to-call links on the site. Quick to check, and until it is checked there is no visibility on website phone enquiries at all.
  3. Stop the "Contact" tag firing on page load. Third distinct week this has been raised. It is now producing 9 of the account's 11 "conversions" and is actively misleading both the weekly report and Google's automated recommendations.
  4. Hold the budget at $15/day. Costs came down on their own and lead volume is currently unmeasurable. There is no case for spending more until the tracking is trustworthy, and no case for spending less while clicks cost $1.05.
  5. Resubmit the new sitemap in Bing Webmaster Tools and Google Search Console if that has not already been done — the file is fixed, but search engines need to be told.
  6. Reconnect Bing Webmaster Tools. Two weeks with no AI-citation data. It only takes a sign-in, and AI citations have been the strongest-performing metric in recent reports.
  7. Free win, still outstanding: add headline and description variants to the active campaign to address the long-standing "Ad strength: poor" flag.

As always, this report made no changes to any campaign, tag, budget, or site setting. Everything above is observation and recommendation only.
