5. What changed & recommended next step

The advertising side of the funnel improved. The website side did not. For the first time in several weeks the ads themselves performed better: more people clicked (75 vs 72), a higher share of people who saw the ad clicked it (8.02% vs 7.06%), and paid visits to the website held steady at 60. The click-through problem flagged in last week's report appears to have corrected itself without anyone touching the account. What did not hold up was what happened after those visitors arrived. Bookings fell from 3 to 2 and phone-call leads from 3 to 2, so the same volume of visitors produced two-thirds as many leads.

That distinction matters, because it changes where to look. When leads fell in previous weeks, traffic fell with them, and the honest explanation was that fewer people were searching. This week traffic was flat and leads still fell. Something between arriving on the site and completing a booking worked less well than it did the week before. That could be entirely random — two bookings versus three is a difference of one person, and at these volumes a single week means very little. It could also be something real on the booking page. The only way to tell them apart is to look.

Cost per booked appointment: $58.83, against a three-week average of $37.81. This is the measure the account was set up to produce, and it is the one worth carrying into any decision about the ads. One week above the average is not a reason to change the budget, especially when the three-week run rate is still under $40 and a prenatal test carries the highest margin in the business. Two consecutive weeks above $50 would be a different conversation.

Recommended next step — one thing, and it takes ten minutes. Go to the website as a customer would and book an appointment: click through from the homepage to the booking page, open Calendly, and complete a test booking. Confirm three things — that the booking page loads and the Calendly widget appears, that a booking can actually be completed, and that the confirmation arrives. Do the same with the phone number on a mobile phone.

This is the right check because the numbers point at the site rather than the campaign, and because two of the three booking-related tags in Google Ads are flagged (Needs attention on the Calendly action, Misconfigured on the older "Book appointment" action). If the booking flow is fine, this week was noise and nothing needs doing. If it is broken or slow, that explains the whole report and is worth fixing immediately. Do not change the budget or the bid strategy this week. Nothing in the campaign data justifies it.

Two housekeeping items. The "Contact" page-load tag improved considerably — down from 11.50 to 5.00 reported conversions — but it still accounts for over half the headline number and still makes that number misleading to anyone reading it cold. And Bing Webmaster Tools has now been signed out for five weeks, which means five weeks of AI-citation data has gone unrecorded — the one place where the site was outperforming its baseline by roughly four times. Signing back in takes a minute and restores the whole trend.

One thing to watch, not act on. ChatGPT referrals fell from 9 sessions to 4, and the visitors who did arrive stayed 3 seconds instead of 7 minutes. That is a large change in a very small number. If it repeats next week it is worth understanding; on its own it is within the range of random variation for a channel this size.
