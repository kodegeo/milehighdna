5. What changed & recommended next step

The story this week is simple: the ads came back on, and for the first time the tracking proves they work. One week of the $15/day search campaign produced 81 visits, 10 measured conversions — including the first Calendly booking ever recorded in Google Ads and a phone call lead — and phone clicks on the site jumped from 3 to 23. Total cost was about $113, so the week's cost per conversion was $11.29, and even judged only on the booking plus the call, roughly $56 per high-intent lead. That is exactly the evidence the Aug 12 ads review was waiting for: unlike the pre-tracking era when money went out with nothing measurable coming back, spend is now traceable to leads. Separately, Bing's AI-citation count accelerated sharply — 90 citations in 30 days versus a baseline of about 27 a month — though almost all cite the homepage, and the two pages built for this purpose (the Denver guide and the Spanish shop page) still are not being cited.

Recommended next step: (1) For the Aug 12 review: keep "Search – DNA Testing Denver" running at $15/day — it is budget-limited (losing ~26% of top-of-page share), so if the goal is more bookings, the cleanest lever is a modest budget increase on this one campaign rather than un-pausing the others. (2) The campaign's "Ad strength: poor" flag is the free improvement available — adding headline/description variants and images could lift CTR without new spend. (3) calendly_open fired 0 times while book_appointment fired twice — worth a quick check that the calendly_open tag still works so the booking funnel stays measurable. As always, this report made no changes to campaigns, tags, or site settings.
