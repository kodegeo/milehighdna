5. What changed & recommended next step

The tracking that broke last week has largely fixed itself, and the picture underneath it is good. Two of the three measurement problems flagged in the last report have resolved. The Calendly booking action moved from Misconfigured back to Needs attention and recorded 4 bookings after a week of zero. The website's tap-to-call event, which had never before read zero, recorded 14 clicks from 10 people. Both were predicted to be tag faults rather than real drops in demand, and both turned out to be exactly that.

The number that matters is $29.81. That is what each booked appointment cost in advertising this week — 4 bookings against $119.22 of spend. This is the first time in three weeks it has been possible to calculate at all, and the last measurable figure, from the week of August 17–23, was $118.80 for one booking. Adding phone calls, the week produced 7 genuine high-intent leads at $17.06 each, against 2 leads at $36.70 last week. For a business where a prenatal test carries the highest margin, spending roughly $30 to land a booked appointment is a workable number. It is one week of data and should not be treated as settled, but it is the first evidence the account has produced that the advertising can pay for itself.

Spend went up, and it bought more. Cost rose 62% to $119.22. That was not a budget change — nothing in the account was touched. Impressions were flat at 826, but clicks rose 40% to 98, meaning the click-through rate jumped from 8.45% to 11.86%. The same ads shown to the same number of people got clicked considerably more often. Cost per click rose modestly to $1.22, still well under half the $3.00 cap. Daily spend averaged $17.03 against a $15/day budget, which is within Google's normal latitude to overspend on individual days.

Two things to keep an eye on. First, the "Contact" tag that fires on page load is now worse than ever — it nearly doubled to 17.50 and accounts for 71% of the account's reported conversions. It is inflating the headline number and feeding Google's automated bidding a page-load counter instead of customers. Second, the Direct channel still shows non-human patterns (9 events per session at 4 seconds), though at roughly a quarter of last week's scale; site-wide average engagement time has recovered from about 6 seconds to 54 seconds as that traffic receded.

And one genuine bright spot outside the ads. Visits arriving from ChatGPT tripled to 10, and those visitors spent an average of 10 minutes 16 seconds on the site — more than eleven times the site average, and more engaged than any other channel by a wide margin. Organic search visits grew for a third straight week (36 → 43 → 59), consistent with the sitemap fix beginning to take effect.

Recommended next steps — conservative, in priority order

  1. Hold everything steady for another week. Nothing was changed this week and the account had its best week on record. The single most useful thing now is a second week of clean cost-per-booked-appointment data to confirm $29.81 is a real number and not a one-week outlier. Resist the urge to raise the budget until there are two comparable weeks.
  2. Finish the booking tag fix. The Calendly action improved to "Needs attention" but is not fully healthy, and the second action, "Book appointment", is still Misconfigured and returned zero. GA4 recorded 6 bookings against the 4 that Google Ads saw, so some bookings are still going unattributed. A developer task on the website tags.
  3. Stop the "Contact" tag firing on page load. Fourth distinct week this has been raised, and it is now at its worst — 17.50 of the account's 24.49 reported conversions. It corrupts the headline figure and misleads Google's automated bidding.
  4. Investigate calendly_open, which has never fired across six reports, despite bookings clearly happening. It is likely a tag that was never wired up rather than one that broke.
  5. Reconnect Bing Webmaster Tools. Three weeks with no AI-citation data. It takes one sign-in, and AI citations have been among the strongest-performing metrics on record — especially relevant now that ChatGPT referrals are growing and are the most engaged traffic on the site.
  6. Free win, still outstanding: add headline and description variants to the active campaign to address the long-standing "Ad strength: poor" flag. With click-through rate now at 11.86%, better ad copy has a clear runway.

As always, this report made no changes to any campaign, tag, budget, or site setting. Everything above is observation and recommendation only.
